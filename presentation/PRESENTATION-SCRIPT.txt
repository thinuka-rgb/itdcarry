SLIDE 1 — Same DNA. Different answer?
Hello, I’m Thinuka. My project is called ITDcarry. It asks: can a different or updated DNA-analysis pipeline still detect the same known FLT3-ITD mutation? ITDcarry tests this using artificial DNA data containing that exact mutation.

SLIDE 2 — One threshold. A different result.
This demo uses the same software with two different settings. Look at the numbers: two and five. Our data has four separate pieces of evidence for the mutation. Requiring two finds it. Requiring five doesn’t report it. This is one example of how a change in the pipeline can change the answer.

SLIDE 3 — Challenge it. Then compare exactly.
There are three steps. First, record the known FLT3-ITD mutation. Second, give the pipeline artificial DNA data containing it. Third, check that the detected mutation matches exactly: its position, its length, and its DNA letters. The details at the bottom describe the mutation used in this demo.

SLIDE 5 — A check on software. A traceable explanation.
Here, changing one setting stopped the pipeline from reporting the known mutation. That shows why we should check whether a changed pipeline can still detect it. We can inspect the output and repeat the test. It tells us about the software, not a person’s health. It cannot diagnose anyone or check laboratory work. This demo ran on my computer; the cloud services listed below weren’t tested here.
